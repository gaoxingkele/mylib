# Domain profiles

## Paper

Preserve IMRaD or the manuscript's actual scholarly structure. Check:

- the introduction narrows from a real gap to a bounded research question;
- every contribution maps to a method component and visible evidence;
- methods are reproducible from supplied material;
- results distinguish observation from interpretation;
- causal, general, and “state of the art” claims match the design and comparisons;
- limitations are specific rather than ritual disclaimers;
- abstract claims appear in the body and conclusion.

Protected spans include citations, BibTeX keys, equations, symbols, statistics, dataset
names, figure or table references, theorem conditions, and calibrated hedges.

## Proposal

Keep justified ambition, but connect every promise to feasibility evidence. Check:

- the opening fixes a concrete need, goal, gap, proposed idea, and payoff;
- aims are independently useful or name a fallback when dependent;
- preliminary results, prior work, collaborators, or staged tests support feasibility;
- future results are not written as completed findings;
- agency-specific headings and page constraints are checked against current official
  instructions rather than memory.

Protected spans include aims, milestones, budgets, dates, preliminary-result numbers,
partner names, and commitments. Never invent a collaborator, letter, result, or track
record.

## Article

Preserve the intended audience and the writer's stance. Check:

- the opening gives a concrete reason to read, not a universal trend formula;
- named sources replace vague authority;
- examples come from supplied notes or verified sources;
- paragraphs advance one argument rather than restating a theme;
- transitions express actual logic, not decoration;
- the ending resolves the article's question without generic uplift.

Do not add “personality” that the writer did not provide. A neutral technical article
may correctly remain neutral.

## Patent

Formal repetition in claims is not an AI signal. Accuracy and legal consistency outrank
stylistic variety. Review four coupled layers:

1. problem: a concrete technical constraint in the prior or actual system;
2. mechanism: data objects, components, state changes, conditions, and interactions;
3. effect: a result caused by that mechanism under stated conditions;
4. support: description, embodiment, drawing, field table, parameter basis, and fallback.

High-risk “AI-like” patent patterns are primarily substantive:

- a list of generic modules with no data or state interaction;
- every subsystem described at the same superficial depth;
- effects attached to labels rather than causal features;
- arbitrary thresholds, dimensions, weights, or scenarios with no engineering basis;
- inconsistent names for the same object or identical prose for different objects;
- embodiments that merely repeat the claims;
- symmetric “collect-process-analyze-output” pipelines lacking exception paths;
- invented implementation details used to make the draft look concrete.

For each independent claim, produce a claim chart with `feature`, `problem addressed`,
`mechanism`, `technical effect`, `description support`, `engineering evidence`, and
`fallback claim`. If a proposed detail lacks pre-filing support, mark it for inventor
confirmation; do not insert it as fact.

Before completing a patent edit, route legal and technical checks to the installed CNIPA
or PAA skills where available. This skill owns authorship, prose, provenance, and
narrative coherence; it does not replace novelty search or grant-risk review.
