## Preserve these rhetorical moves

- Start from a concrete task ambiguity (status, time, join, projection), then
  identify the information needed to resolve it. Avoid broad AI-history openings.
- Give each paragraph one job: observation, explanation, comparison, boundary,
  or next test. Use the preceding paragraph's unresolved issue as the transition.
- State findings directly, then give counts and the comparator. Explain the
  interpretation with one necessary boundary; do not append the same disclaimer
  to every sentence or paragraph.
- Prefer concrete subjects and verbs: the selector retains, the witness rejects,
  the comparison isolates. Avoid nominal stacks and promotional adjectives.
- Distinguish observed behavior from explanation: a changed error composition
  does not itself identify why each query changed. Use a testable next step.
- A short contrast sentence may summarize a longer technical paragraph.
  Vary length according to function; never impose uniform word counts.
- Preserve terminology and numerical precision already established locally.
  Introduce a new term only when it names a new measurable distinction.

## Integration check for new experiments

Does the new paragraph answer an existing question? Are method, denominator,
comparison and visibility status named? Is the explanation supported rather
than merely plausible? Can one redundant caveat be removed without changing
the scope? Does the text flow into the surrounding human-polished paragraph?

Retain the original title, authors and voice unless the user changes them.
Do not import whole older AI-written sections merely because their evidence
is reusable. Re-express verified evidence in the current manuscript's voice.
No detector-evasion targets or artificial sentence-length distributions.
